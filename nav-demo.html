// The throughline

Train it, attack it, build it.

I work at an intersection that usually gets split apart, and the seam between those disciplines is where I do my best work. At xAI and OpenAI I have spent thousands of hours evaluating model outputs across text, image, and video, reaching the highest quality tier and building a working map of how these systems fail. As an independent researcher I turn that understanding on live systems, finding and reporting real vulnerabilities.

What connects all three is honest evaluation. On the training side, every shortcut in judgment compounds into model behavior. On the attack side, most "secure" AI is only secure against the demos its team ran. On the build side, honesty has to be a structural property of the system, not a vibe.

Before AI and security, I spent years behind the camera and leading creative work: assistant photographer, freelance photojournalist, then creative director at Home Depot planning and shooting editorial and program photography. Photojournalism and creative direction are both disciplines of honest evaluation, documenting what is actually there and judging whether work holds up rather than whether it impresses. Different medium, same throughline.
