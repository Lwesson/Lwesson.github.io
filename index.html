  • TTB Label Verifier

    AI Product

    An AI-assisted compliance tool built as a federal take-home. It reads an alcohol label photo, compares every mandatory field to the application, and checks the government health warning against 27 CFR part 16, in under five seconds per label or hundreds at a time in a batch. Uncertain reads land in Review or Unreadable, so a person decides the hard cases instead of the model guessing.

    I benchmarked two vision models on a 15-label test set and shipped the smaller one. Both scored 14 of 15, but the smaller model was faster and transcribed text verbatim, while the larger one silently corrected capitalization that the regulation depends on. 115 automated tests.

  • Cold Open, a daily security puzzle

    Game

    Three generated challenges a day, in cipher breaking, log forensics, and network traffic analysis, about five minutes in all. Each day's set is derived from the date, so every player gets the same puzzles with no server behind it, and there is no answer key to look up, only the method. After every puzzle, solved or missed, it explains the technique and where it shows up in real incidents.

    Every puzzle has exactly one defensible answer, and the test suite enforces that, along with every claim a hint makes about the puzzle. A generator that lets a harmless row pass for a second incident marks a correct analyst wrong. 44 automated tests.

// The throughline

Train it, attack it, build it.

I work at an intersection that usually gets split apart, and the seam between those disciplines is where I do my best work. At xAI and OpenAI I have spent thousands of hours evaluating model outputs across text, image, and video, reaching the highest quality tier and building a working map of how these systems fail. As an independent researcher I turn that understanding on live systems, finding and reporting real vulnerabilities, and I consult as a paid subject-matter expert on AI and security for investment research.

What connects all three is honest evaluation. On the training side, every shortcut in judgment compounds into model behavior. On the attack side, most "secure" AI is only secure against the demos its team ran. On the build side, honesty has to be a structural property of the system, not a vibe.

Before AI and security, I spent years behind the camera and leading creative work: assistant photographer, freelance photojournalist, then creative director of my own practice, planning and shooting editorial and program photography for clients including Home Depot. Photojournalism and creative direction are both disciplines of honest evaluation, documenting what is actually there and judging whether work holds up rather than whether it impresses. Different medium, same throughline.
