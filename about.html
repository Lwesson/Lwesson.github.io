About

Train it, attack it, build it.

I work at an intersection that usually gets split apart, and the seam between those disciplines is where I do my best work.

Recent Focus

The throughline

I train and evaluate frontier AI models, I test AI-powered and enterprise systems adversarially as a vulnerability researcher, and I build the security tooling that ties the two together.

At xAI and OpenAI I have spent thousands of hours evaluating model outputs across text, image, and video, reaching the highest quality tier and building a working map of how these systems fail. As an independent researcher I turn that understanding on live systems, finding and reporting real vulnerabilities. And I build platforms that make the whole loop repeatable.

What connects all three is honest evaluation. On the training side, every shortcut in judgment compounds into model behavior. On the attack side, most "secure" AI is only secure against the demos its team ran. On the build side, honesty has to be a structural property of the system, not a vibe. That conviction is the throughline of everything here.

History

Before AI and security

Before AI and security, I spent years behind the camera and leading creative work. I started as an assistant photographer and a freelance photojournalist, then grew into a creative director for Home Depot, where I planned and shot editorial and program photography and managed a team and talent.

Along the way I shot for Johnson & Johnson, Washington State University, and the Olympic College Foundation, and made brand and event work for HTC featuring Robinson Cano and Pyramid Brewing in Seattle. Earlier, from 2005 to 2008, I managed projects for a facilities company contracted to the U.S. Navy and Air Force.
